How this outlook works
  1. The page downloads forecast PM2.5 (fine particles) and wind for 11 places in Indonesia and Malaysia, plus the wind over Singapore.
  2. PM2.5 is averaged over the next 24 hours and converted to a PSI-equivalent using NEA's 24-hour PM2.5 bands (PSI 100 is about 55 µg/m³).
  3. For each place it checks whether the wind at that place and over Singapore both point from there toward Singapore: within about 45° counts as "toward", within about 70° as "partly toward".
  4. The outlook is High if a place at PSI-equivalent 100 or more is strongly upwind and could arrive within 48 hours at current wind speeds. It is Moderate if the wind is only partly favourable, or a place at 75 or more is strongly upwind. Otherwise it is Low.
This is a simple rule-based indicator, not a trained model, and its thresholds have not yet been tested against past haze events. The data are model forecasts on a coarse grid of about 45 km, the places are sample points rather than fire locations, and real haze transport depends on winds at several heights. For official information, check NEA and the ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre.
How PSI is shown at outdoor recreation areas
Each recreation area gets a marker coloured by its region's predicted PSI, with the number inside. Courts and playgrounds from OpenStreetMap use the same colours as small dots, football fields as squares and sports complexes as diamonds. The activity guide is a simplified version of NEA's PSI health advisory bands, so check nea.gov.sg for official advice.
Click anywhere on the map, or tap a recreation area, court or playground, to see the predicted PSI for that spot.

Five linear regression models, one per NEA region, trained on Admiralty weather station data and each region's PSI, April 2014 to November 2017 (n = 979 days per region). Test MAE versus a naive baseline (tomorrow = today): North 5.71 vs 6.07, South 6.16 vs 6.68, East 5.84 vs 6.33, West 6.12 vs 6.62, Central 6.88 vs 7.44.

Limitations: PSI is reported per region, not per street, so any spot you pick gets its region's prediction. Region borders are approximated by whichever NEA region centre is nearest. The clean map is a simplified outline, not a survey-grade map. All regions use the same weather inputs. The live version uses the current instant weather reading in place of the daily summaries the models were trained on. The listed recreation areas are examples with approximate positions. Court, playground, football field and sports complex locations come from OpenStreetMap volunteers and may be incomplete, and the sports complex layer can include some indoor venues. When you zoom in, the town bubbles show the predicted value of the region each town belongs to, because the models predict one value per region.

Wind speeds from NEA's live feed are reported in knots and are converted to km/h to match the units the models were trained on. The experimental "nearest weather station" option gives each location the readings from its nearest NEA station instead of the island-wide average; it has not been validated.

The regional haze outlook is a separate rule-based indicator and does not change the model predictions above, which use local weather only. When the outlook is Moderate or High, those predictions may underestimate tomorrow's PSI.
